### job-scheduler  "Job Scheduler (delayed and recurring jobs on worker threads)"  kind: concurrency
Evidence: B job-scheduler (13 companies: Amazon, LinkedIn, Salesforce, Rubrik, Uber, Oracle, Brex, Microsoft, Netflix, Lyft, Rippling, Confluent, Instacart); I job-scheduler (Swiggy cron, MPL, Oracle, Media.net, Uber, Harness, Arcesium); A job-scheduler-durable (Cursor, Decagon, OpenAI, Scale AI).
Root: Scheduler owning a min-heap of ScheduledJob ordered by nextRunAt, one ReentrantLock + Condition, one dispatcher thread and a fixed worker pool (jobs never run on the dispatcher). API: schedule(job, delay) -> id; scheduleFixedRate / scheduleFixedDelay; cancel(id); shutdown(graceful | now). Keep the core testable: a pure `pollDue(now)` step driven by the dispatcher, so tests use an injected Clock without sleeping. The dispatcher waits `awaitNanos(head.due - now)` in a loop and is signalled when a job earlier than the head arrives (the lost-wakeup trap). Job life: SCHEDULED -> RUNNING -> DONE | FAILED | CANCELLED; cancel vs dispatch decided under the lock (exactly one wins; a cancelled job never starts). Recurring: fixed rate re-enqueues at dispatch, fixed delay after the run ends; never two runs of the same recurring job at once (overlap policy). Retries: a RetryPolicy (exponential backoff with jitter, max attempts) re-enqueues at now+backoff. Rules that change: next-run policy (fixed rate, fixed delay, a simple cron), retry policy, overlap policy, which due job first (priority). O(1)/O(log n): heap for due order, map id -> job for cancel (lazy removal flag in the heap). Tests: due order; an earlier insert wakes the dispatcher; cancel before run never runs; cancel racing dispatch: exactly one wins; fixed delay never overlaps; retries with backoff then FAILED; at most N jobs run at once; a throwing job does not kill the dispatcher; shutdown drains or cancels per policy. Say what the JDK already has (ScheduledThreadPoolExecutor, DelayQueue) and why the interviewer wants it built. PREREQUISITES are part of this page (LinkedIn, Salesforce and others ask for "delayed, recurring and prerequisite tasks"): a job may name jobs it waits for; keep a remaining-prerequisite count per job and a dependents list; a job becomes due only when its count reaches zero (the race: two prerequisites finishing at once both decrement: exactly one enqueues it); a failed prerequisite skips its transitive dependents; a cycle is refused at submit time with its path (Kahn's algorithm / DFS colours). Give this its own section of moves and 2-3 follow-up cards. Twists: misfire after downtime (run once / run all / skip), persistence with many scheduler instances (jobs table, claim by conditional UPDATE with a lease; at-least-once, so jobs must be idempotent), time zones in cron, per-type concurrency limits.
